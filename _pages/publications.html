Working Papers

[1] Leveraging Synthetic Data from Large Language Models for Machine Learning in Marketing Applications: Target-Aware Minimum Class Confusion Method (with Heiner Evanschitzky (Manchester); Hai-Anh Tran (Manchester); Charles H. Noble (Tennessee))
Current stage: Ready to Submit, Job Market Paper
  • Abstract: This study investigates whether synthetic data from Large Language Models can effectively represent real-world data and how it can be leveraged in machine learning applications for marketing research and practices. Through comprehensive analysis of eight public datasets using embeddings visualization, quantitative metrics, and task-level evaluations, we find significant distributional gaps between synthetic and real data. To address this, we propose Target-Aware Minimum Class Confusion (TAMCC), a novel domain adaptation method that combines synthetic data with limited labeled real data. TAMCC consistently outperforms existing methods, achieving 81.00% average accuracy with just 200 real samples—only 6.91% below models trained on complete full real datasets. Our contributions include a comprehensive evaluation of LLM-generated synthetic data and a actionable method, TAMCC, for leveraging synthetic data in machine learning for marketing research and practice.
